## B2 Materiality

**Purpose:** to set the amounts that guide the scope of work and the evaluation of misstatements.

| ID | Step | Evidence expected | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2.01 | Select the benchmark that users focus on most, and record why. | Benchmark and rationale | ISA 320 |
| B2.02 | Apply a percentage to the benchmark to set overall materiality. Record the reason for the percentage chosen. | Calculation | ISA 320 |
| B2.03 | Set performance materiality below overall materiality, reflecting the expected level of misstatement, the control environment and past experience. | Calculation and rationale | ISA 320 |
| B2.04 | Set the amount below which misstatements are clearly trivial and need not be accumulated. | Threshold | ISA 450 |
| B2.05 | Consider whether any item needs a lower, specific materiality, such as related-party transactions or directors' remuneration. | Specific materiality, if any | ISA 320 |
| B2.06 | At completion, reassess materiality against the final figures and revise if the benchmark has moved significantly. Consider the effect on work already done. | Reassessment note | ISA 320 |

**Guide ranges built into the calculator.** These are common practice, not requirements of any standard, and are a reviewer decision.

| Benchmark | Typical use | Proposed range |
|---|---|---|
| Profit before tax | Profit-oriented entity with stable profits | 5-10% |
| Revenue | Low or volatile profit; trading entities | 0.5-2% |
| Total assets | Asset-holding or investment entities | 1-2% |
| Equity | Entities where solvency is the focus | 2-5% |

Performance materiality: proposed 50-75% of overall materiality (lower end for first-year audits, weak controls or a history of adjustments). Trivial threshold: proposed up to 5% of overall materiality.

## Sampling table (reviewer decision R5: a proposal)

This is a simplified, judgement-based method. The factors below are a proposal drawn from common practice, not taken from ISA 530, which does not prescribe sample sizes.

**Step 1.** Remove and test in full: every item above performance materiality, and any item that is unusual, old or related-party.

**Step 2.** For the remaining population:

> Sample size = (remaining population value ÷ performance materiality) × risk factor, rounded up

| Assessed risk for the assertion | Factor, no other substantive evidence | Factor, where analytical or other procedures also give evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 1.0 | 0.5 |
| Moderate | 2.0 | 1.0 |
| High or significant | 3.0 | 2.0 |

**Step 3.** Apply limits: minimum 5 items where any sample is taken; if the result exceeds 60, reconsider the approach (stratify, or use analytical procedures) instead of capping silently.

**Step 4.** Select so that every item has a chance of selection: random or systematic selection for representative samples. Record the method.

**Step 5.** Evaluate errors: investigate the nature and cause of each, project sample errors to the population, and compare the projected misstatement with performance materiality. Carry it to D1.

**Tests of controls, where used.** Proposed sizes by how often the control operates: annual 1; quarterly 2; monthly 3; weekly 8; daily 20; many times a day 25. Any deviation means the control cannot be relied on without further work.
