# Section C: Fieldwork, higher-risk areas

**Status:** AI draft v0.1, 12 November 2026. Not reviewed. Not approved for use on any engagement.

Covers C13 Revenue, C16 Journal entries and management override, C4 Inventories, C7 Related parties. C5 Trade receivables is in the main library file.

References are at standard level and from memory. Tiers: **Core** (all risk levels), **Ext** (assertions rated moderate or high), **Sig** (assertions flagged as significant risk). Assertion codes: E existence/occurrence, C completeness, A accuracy, V valuation, R rights and obligations, CO cut-off, CL classification, P presentation.

Accounting requirements are worded neutrally ("under the framework"). IFRS for SMEs has been revised, so the reviewer should check each accounting point against the edition the client applies.

## C13 Revenue

**Audit objective:** to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence that recorded revenue arose from real transactions, is complete, is measured accurately, is recorded in the correct period, and is properly classified and disclosed.

**Risks commonly found in small entities**
- Revenue recorded early, or fictitious sales, to meet targets, covenants or valuation expectations.
- Revenue understated to reduce tax, especially cash sales.
- Long-term or milestone contracts recognised on an inappropriate basis.
- Sales to related parties on unusual terms.
- Discounts, rebates and returns not accrued.

| ID | Tier | Assertions | Procedure | Evidence expected | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C13.01 | Core | A, CL | Obtain a breakdown of revenue by stream and by month. Agree it to the trial balance. For each stream, record the recognition policy and conclude whether it complies with the framework. | Breakdown; policy assessment per stream | ISA 500, ISA 315 |
| C13.02 | Core | E, C, A | Build an expectation of revenue for each stream from independent or audited data, such as volumes and prices, contracts, headcount or capacity. Compare with recorded revenue, set a threshold, and corroborate differences above it. | Expectation, threshold, differences, corroboration | ISA 520 |
| C13.03 | Core | E, A | Select recorded sales and trace each to order or contract, delivery or service evidence, invoice, and receipt of cash. | Sample schedule with documents | ISA 500, ISA 530 |
| C13.04 | Core | C | Select from a source outside the sales ledger, such as delivery notes, job records or contracts, and trace each into recorded revenue. | Sample schedule | ISA 500 |
| C13.05 | Core | CO | Test sales and credit notes either side of the period end against delivery or service evidence. Shared with C5.07. | Cut-off schedule | ISA 500 |
| C13.06 | Core | C, A | Reconcile revenue per the ledger to revenue reported in VAT returns for the period. Investigate differences. | Reconciliation | ISA 500 |
| C13.07 | Core | P | Confirm that revenue disclosures, including the policy and any disaggregation required, agree to the evidence and meet the framework. | Disclosure tie-out | ISA 330 |
| C13.08 | Ext | E, A | Analyse gross margin by month and by product or customer. Investigate months or lines that depart from the pattern. | Margin analysis | ISA 520 |

## C16 Journal entries and management override

**Audit objective:** to respond to the risk that management overrides controls to misstate the financial statements. This risk is significant on every engagement, so all procedures are Core. The reviewer should confirm that C16.01-C16.08 cover what ISA 240 requires on every audit.

| ID | Tier | Assertions | Procedure | Evidence expected | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C16.01 | Core | All | Understand how journal entries are initiated, authorised and posted, who has access, and whether entries can be changed or deleted after posting. Uses B1.07. | Process notes | ISA 240, ISA 315 |
| C16.02 | Core | C | Obtain the full population of journal entries for the period, including post-closing and consolidation-type adjustments. Prove completeness by rolling opening balances forward to closing balances. | Journal listing; completeness check | ISA 240 |
| C16.03 | Core | All | Ask staff involved in financial reporting whether they were asked to make unusual entries or noticed inappropriate ones. | Inquiry record | ISA 240 |
| C16.04 | Core | All | Select entries for testing using risk characteristics. This needs the journal listing imported into the product (added to the build plan); until then, apply the filters to an exported listing. Filter for: entries at or after the period end; entries posted by senior management or unusual users; round amounts; entries with no description; unusual account pairings, such as revenue against anything other than receivables or cash; entries to seldom-used accounts; entries posted at weekends or outside working hours. | Selection criteria and results | ISA 240 |
| C16.05 | Core | All | For each selected entry, inspect support, confirm authorisation, and conclude whether it has a valid business reason and is correctly recorded. | Schedule with support | ISA 240 |
| C16.06 | Core | All | Consider whether entries during the period, not only at its end, also need testing, and record the decision. | Decision note | ISA 240 |
| C16.07 | Core | V | Review accounting estimates for bias. Consider whether judgements, taken together, lean in one direction, and compare prior-year estimates with outcomes. Uses the retrospective reviews in each area. | Bias assessment across estimates | ISA 240, ISA 540 |
| C16.08 | Core | All | Identify significant transactions outside the normal course of business or otherwise unusual. Evaluate whether the business rationale, or lack of one, suggests fraud or concealment. | Schedule of unusual transactions with evaluation | ISA 240 |
| C16.09 | Core | All | Conclude on whether evidence indicates override, and whether the fraud risk assessment needs revising. Report suspected fraud as required. | Conclusion; link to D6 | ISA 240 |

## C4 Inventories

**Audit objective:** to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence that inventories exist, are owned, are complete, are measured at the lower of cost and the framework's recoverable measure, and are properly disclosed.

**Risks commonly found in small entities**
- No perpetual records; quantities depend entirely on the year-end count.
- Slow-moving, damaged or obsolete items not written down.
- Cost built up incorrectly: overheads wrongly included or omitted.
- Goods held for others, or held by others, wrongly included or excluded.
- Cut-off errors between purchases, sales and the count.

| ID | Tier | Assertions | Procedure | Evidence expected | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C4.01 | Core | C, A | Obtain the final inventory listing by item with quantity, unit cost and value. Check its arithmetic and agree the total to the trial balance. | Listing; agreement to TB | ISA 500 |
| C4.02 | Core | E, C | Where inventory is material, attend the physical count. Evaluate management's count instructions, observe that they are followed, and inspect the condition of goods. If attendance is impracticable, perform alternative procedures and record why. | Count attendance memo | ISA 501 |
| C4.03 | Core | E, C | At the count, perform test counts in both directions: from the count sheets to the floor, and from the floor to the count sheets. Record the items for later tracing. | Test count schedule | ISA 501 |
| C4.04 | Core | E, C | Trace your test counts and a selection of count sheets into the final inventory listing. Investigate differences. | Tracing schedule | ISA 501 |
| C4.05 | Core | E, C | If the count was held on a date other than the period end, test the roll-forward or roll-back of movements between the two dates. | Roll-forward testing | ISA 501 |
| C4.06 | Core | CO | Record the last goods-received and dispatch documents at the count. Confirm that purchases and sales either side are recorded in the correct period and consistent with what was counted. | Cut-off schedule | ISA 501, ISA 500 |
| C4.07 | Core | A, V | Test unit costs for a selection of items to supplier invoices, and confirm the cost formula used is permitted and applied consistently. | Costing test | ISA 500 |
| C4.08 | Core | V | Test for write-downs: compare cost with selling prices after the period end less costs to sell for a selection, and review ageing or last-movement reports for slow-moving items. Assess management's allowance. | Recoverable-amount test; ageing review | ISA 540, ISA 500 |
| C4.09 | Core | R, E | Ask about, and look for evidence of, goods held on behalf of others, consignment stock, and goods held at third-party locations. Confirm third-party holdings directly or inspect them. | Inquiry; confirmations | ISA 501, ISA 505 |
| C4.10 | Core | P | Confirm disclosures: policy, cost formula, carrying amounts by category, write-downs, and inventory pledged as security. | Disclosure tie-out | ISA 330 |
| C4.11 | Ext | A, V | For manufactured goods, test the build-up of cost: materials, labour and overheads. Confirm overhead absorption is based on normal capacity and excludes selling and administration costs. | Cost build-up test | ISA 500, ISA 540 |
| C4.12 | Ext | E, C, V | Analyse inventory days and gross margin by product line against the prior year. Investigate unexpected movements. | Analytical review | ISA 520 |
| C4.13 | Ext | V | Compare last year's write-down allowance with what was actually sold or scrapped. | Retrospective review | ISA 540 |
| C4.14 | Ext | A | Test the accuracy of the ageing or last-movement report used for the write-down assessment. | Report accuracy test | ISA 500 |
| C4.15 | Sig | E | Extend count coverage: attend all significant locations, increase test counts, and open sealed packages or check for hollow stacks on a sample basis. Consider unannounced counts. | Extended count records | ISA 240, ISA 501 |
| C4.16 | Sig | E, V | For high-value items, verify identity and quality, using an expert where the nature of the goods needs one. | Verification record; link to B11 | ISA 620 |
| C4.17 | Sig | E, C | Review adjustments made to inventory records after the count and journals posted to inventory near the period end. | Adjustment review; link to C16 | ISA 240 |

## C7 Related-party balances and transactions

**Audit objective:** to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence that related-party relationships and transactions have been identified, accounted for and disclosed as the framework requires, and that they do not conceal fraud or misstate the financial statements.

**Risks commonly found in small entities**
- Owner's personal transactions passed through the business.
- Loans to or from owners and sister companies with no agreement, no terms and doubtful recoverability.
- Transactions at prices that differ from market, with tax and transfer-pricing consequences.
- Related parties not identified or not disclosed.
- Balances between group or sister entities that do not agree.

Completeness is the central concern, so this area is never scoped out on size.

| C7.03 | Core | E, A, R | Confirm balances directly with each related party, or agree to the counterparty's records where you also audit it. Reconcile differences. | Confirmations; reconciliations | ISA 505, ISA 550 |
| C7.04 | Core | A, V | For significant transactions, inspect agreements and supporting documents. Understand the terms, including pricing, interest, repayment and security. | Documents; terms summary | ISA 550 |
| C7.05 | Core | V | Assess the recoverability of amounts due from related parties, considering the counterparty's financial position and payment history. | Recoverability assessment | ISA 540, ISA 550 |
| C7.06 | Core | CL, P | Confirm that owner current accounts, loans and trading balances are correctly classified between current and non-current, and between liabilities and equity. | Classification review | ISA 500 |
