## 1. Engagement letter template

{Firm letterhead}

{Date}

To: {Addressee: owners, board or manager}, {Client legal name}

**Audit of the financial statements for the year ending {period end}**

You have asked us to audit the financial statements of {Client legal name} ("the Company") for the year ending {period end}. This letter sets out what the engagement covers and what each of us is responsible for. Please read it and sign the copy to confirm your agreement.

**1. What we will do**

The financial statements we will audit are the Company's statement of financial position at {period end}, its statements of comprehensive income, changes in equity and cash flows for the year, and the notes with the accounting policies. We will report whether, in all material respects, those statements are prepared in accordance with {framework}.

**2. How we will do it**

We will carry out the audit under International Standards on Auditing. We will keep to the ethical rules that apply, including those on independence, and we will plan and perform the work so that we can obtain reasonable assurance that the statements as a whole contain no material misstatement, whether from fraud or error.

Reasonable assurance is a high degree of comfort, not a promise. We test and we use judgement, and every system of internal control has limits, so a material misstatement can stay undetected even in an audit that is properly planned and carried out. The risk is greater for fraud than for error, since fraud can involve collusion, forgery or deliberate concealment.

In doing the audit we will:
- identify and assess where the statements could be materially misstated, and design our procedures in response;
- learn enough about the Company's internal control to choose suitable procedures. We will not be examining that control in order to give an opinion on it;
- consider the accounting policies, whether the estimates are reasonable, and how the statements are presented as a whole;
- decide whether it is appropriate for the statements to be prepared on the going concern basis.

If we find significant weaknesses in internal control during the audit, we will tell you about them in writing.

**3. What you are responsible for**

We carry out the audit on the understanding that management and, where applicable, those charged with governance accept that they are responsible for:

(a) the preparation and fair presentation of the financial statements in accordance with {framework};

(b) keeping the internal control that management judges necessary so that the statements contain no material misstatement from fraud or error;

(c) judging whether the Company can continue as a going concern, and disclosing what that requires;

(d) providing us with:
- all the information, such as records and documents, that bears on the statements;
- anything more that we ask for;
- free access to the people in the Company from whom we need evidence.

At the end of the audit we will ask you to confirm in writing the statements you have made to us during it.

**[If the firm also provides accounting or tax services]**
**4. Other services**

We also provide {description of other services} to the Company. Those services are covered by a separate letter. In providing them we do not take management decisions; you remain responsible for the accounting records, for reviewing and approving anything we prepare, and for the financial statements.

**5. Our report**

We expect to issue a report that gives our opinion on the financial statements. What we find may change the form or content of that report. If it does, we will talk to you about the reasons before the report is issued.

**6. Timing and information**

We plan to carry out our work in {fieldwork period} and to issue our report by {target date}, provided we receive the information listed in our request list by {information date} and your staff are available to answer queries.

**7. Fees**

Our fee for the audit is {fee and basis}, plus applicable VAT, payable {payment terms}. It is based on the information you have given us and on the records being complete and available on time. If significant additional work proves necessary, we will discuss it with you before carrying it out.

**8. Confidentiality and data**

We keep your information confidential, except where law, regulation or professional standards require disclosure. Your data is held in our audit system and stored in {data location}. **[If AI features are enabled]** Our system uses artificial-intelligence tools to assist with drafting and analysis. All such output is reviewed by our staff, and your data is not used to train those tools.

**9. Other terms**

{Firm standard terms: limitation of liability, retention of working papers, anti-money-laundering obligations, complaints, termination, governing law. To be supplied by the firm and reviewed by a lawyer.}

**10. Agreement**

This letter remains in force for future years unless it is replaced or the engagement ends. Please sign and return the attached copy to confirm that it reflects your understanding.

Yours faithfully,

{Partner name}, {Firm name}

Acknowledged and agreed on behalf of {Client legal name}:

Name: ____________ Position: ____________ Signature: ____________ Date: ____________

## 3. Fraud risk factor prompt list (B4.04)

For each factor, the auditor answers Present / Not present / Not applicable, with a comment where present. A factor being present does not mean fraud exists; it is an input to the risk assessment. Wording is tailored to small, owner-managed entities.

### Fraudulent financial reporting

**Incentives and pressures**
1. The business is seeking, or must renew, bank finance, and results affect the outcome.
2. Loan covenants are close to being breached.
3. The owner intends to sell the business or admit an investor, so reported results affect price.
4. Profit, cash flow or liquidity has deteriorated, or losses threaten the business.
5. The owner has given personal guarantees for the Company's debts.
6. Management's pay or bonuses depend heavily on results.
7. There is an incentive to understate profit, for example to reduce tax or distributions to a partner.
8. A licence, tender qualification or visa quota depends on reported figures.
9. A parent, head office or outside shareholder sets demanding targets.

**Opportunities**
10. One person dominates management with no effective oversight.
11. Significant transactions with related parties, especially ones not audited by us.
12. Significant estimates involving judgement that is hard to corroborate.
13. Significant, unusual or complex transactions, particularly near the period end.
14. Accounting staff are few, inexperienced or frequently replaced.
15. Journal entries can be posted or changed without review; the accounting software has weak access controls or no audit trail.
16. Significant bank accounts or operations in other jurisdictions with no clear business reason.
17. Business is conducted through intermediaries without evident purpose.

**Attitudes and rationalisations**
18. Management shows little regard for controls, record-keeping or regulatory obligations.
19. Known history of breaches of law, tax penalties or disputes with authorities.
20. No clear distinction between the owner's personal affairs and the Company's.
21. Management is aggressive or unrealistic about forecasts and accounting treatments, or repeatedly justifies questionable accounting on grounds of immateriality.
22. Strained relationship with the current or previous auditor: frequent disputes, unreasonable deadlines, restricted access to people or information, or attempts to influence the scope of work.
23. Frequent changes of auditor, accountant or lawyer.

### Misappropriation of assets

**Incentives and pressures**
24. Employees with access to cash or easily sold assets are known to be under personal financial pressure.
25. Poor relations between the Company and such employees: expected redundancies, pay cuts, delayed salaries, or resentment over treatment.

**Opportunities**
26. Large amounts of cash held or handled.
27. Inventory that is small, valuable or in high demand.
28. Easily convertible assets such as fuel, scrap, vouchers or portable equipment.
29. The same person handles receipts, banking and recording, or orders, receives and pays.
30. Bank reconciliations are late, not reviewed, or prepared by the person who handles cash.
31. No independent check of supplier or employee master data changes.
32. Poor physical security over cash, inventory or equipment.
33. No requirement to take leave for staff in key control positions.
34. Incomplete or late recording of transactions; weak documentation, for example for credit notes and refunds.
35. Owner or senior staff use company cards or accounts with no review of expenses.

**Attitudes and rationalisations**
36. Tolerance of petty theft or of ignoring controls.
37. Changes in an employee's behaviour or lifestyle that suggest assets may have been taken.

### Conditions noticed during the audit
To be reconsidered at completion.

38. Missing, altered or photocopied documents where originals should exist.
39. Unexplained reconciling items or differences between records and confirmations.
40. Vague, inconsistent or implausible answers from management or staff.
41. Unusual delays in providing information, or access denied to records, staff, customers or suppliers.
42. Last-minute adjustments that significantly affect results.
43. Transactions without clear business purpose, or not recorded in the normal way.
44. Tips or complaints about alleged fraud.

## 4. Going-concern indicator list (B7.01)

For each indicator, the auditor answers Present / Not present, with a comment where present. Some are answered automatically from the trial balance and are marked **(auto)**. One or more indicators being present does not by itself mean a material uncertainty exists; it triggers the further work in D4.

### Financial
1. Net liabilities **(auto)**.
2. Net current liabilities **(auto)**.
3. Loss for the year, or losses in successive years **(auto)**.
4. Negative operating cash flow **(auto)**.
5. Borrowings due for repayment within twelve months with no realistic prospect of renewal or repayment.
6. Heavy reliance on short-term borrowing to fund long-term assets.
7. Breach, or likely breach, of loan covenants.
8. Indications that lenders or other creditors are withdrawing support, or suppliers moving to cash-on-delivery terms.
9. Inability to pay creditors on time: overdue supplier balances, delayed salaries, unpaid tax or statutory dues **(partly auto, from ageing)**.
10. Bounced cheques or missed loan instalments.
11. Deteriorating key ratios: liquidity, gearing, interest cover **(auto)**.
12. Significant decline in the value of assets used to generate cash.
13. Dependence on owner or related-party funding that is not formally committed.
14. Significant receivable from a related party or major customer whose recovery is doubtful.
15. Inability to obtain finance for essential investment or new projects.
16. Dividends or owner drawings in excess of profits, or arrears of dividends.

### Operating
17. Management intends to liquidate the Company or cease trading.
18. Loss of key management without replacement.
19. Loss of a major customer, market, contract, franchise, agency or licence.
20. Loss of a principal supplier, or shortages of important supplies.
21. Labour difficulties or loss of key staff.
22. Emergence of a strong competitor, or a significant fall in demand or prices.
23. Dependence on one customer, supplier, product or project.
24. Expiry of the premises lease with no renewal arranged.

### Legal, regulatory and other
25. Trade licence, permits or registrations expired, at risk, or dependent on conditions not met.
26. Non-compliance with capital or other statutory requirements, including accumulated losses reaching a level at which law requires the owners to act. Reviewer to add the local company-law trigger.
27. Pending legal or regulatory proceedings that could result in claims the Company cannot meet.
28. Changes in law, regulation or government policy expected to affect the Company adversely.
29. Uninsured or under-insured losses.
30. Significant tax assessments or penalties.

### Mitigating factors to record
Where indicators are present, note any of the following, which feed D4:
- signed commitment of support from owners or a parent, with evidence of ability to provide it;
- confirmed renewal or new facilities from lenders;
- realistic plans to dispose of assets, reduce costs or raise capital;
- order book or contracts secured after the period end;
- cash received after the period end.

**Auditor's responsibilities**

**[If the opinion is unmodified, qualified or adverse]**

Our job is to form an opinion on these financial statements and to report it. To do that we seek reasonable assurance that they contain no material misstatement, whether from fraud or error.

Reasonable assurance is a high degree of comfort but not a promise: an audit that is properly carried out can still miss a material misstatement. We treat a misstatement as material when it is large enough to change what a reader of the statements would decide.

Throughout the audit we exercised professional judgement and kept a questioning mind. In particular:

- we assessed where the statements might be materially misstated through fraud or error, then chose and carried out procedures aimed at those risks;
- we learned enough about the Company's internal control to choose suitable procedures, and we did not examine that control in order to give an opinion on it;
- we examined the accounting policies, management's estimates and the matching disclosures, and judged whether they are reasonable;
- we considered whether management is right to use the going concern basis, and whether the evidence shows a material uncertainty about going concern. Where we find one, this report must point to the disclosure of it or, if that disclosure falls short, our opinion must be modified;
- we looked at the statements as a whole, including the disclosures, and asked whether they show the underlying transactions and events fairly.

We also report to those charged with governance on the scope and timing we planned and on our significant findings, including any significant weaknesses in internal control that we found.
